One sentence, two simulated futures

“Move 700 XR-17 from Mumbai to the Pune site.”

If you meant Pune Site 1

1200 to 1900

Blocked: 400 over the site’s capacity of 1500

If you meant Pune Site 2

300 to 1000

Valid: Mumbai stays above safety stock

Nothing has moved. The agent asks the question whose answer changes the outcome: “Site 1 would exceed capacity by 400 units; Site 2 remains valid. Which one do you mean?”

Above the line is what was said. Below it, hatched, is what it could do: both readings simulated against a frozen copy of the ERP. The question is chosen by ambiguity times how differently the futures turn out times risk, so MIRROR asks only when the answer matters. These are the real numbers from the demo world.
